Topo maps

A piece of the real world in relief: the ground from satellite elevation data, the sea, lakes and rivers in blue, the forest in greens by tree species, a marker on every mine, and a pin standing on each place you name.

Where

A town, a peak, a lake, an address — anything OpenStreetMap can find — or a latitude, longitude straight off a map app. Add the region if the name is common. The map is looked up again when you click out of the box, not on every key.

Empty, the map is fitted round the pins with a margin. Name a place to centre it there instead, and say how much ground it covers — a valley is 10–30 km, a city and its coast 50, an island or a region a few hundred.

The map

Width
150 mm
Height stretch
×1.5

Real ground is flat at any size a printer makes: a 2,000 m mountain on a 20 km map, 150 mm wide, is 15 mm tall. So the heights are stretched; ×1 is true to scale. The relief never passes 60 mm, and the readout says when it was held back.

Base
3 mm

The sea sits flat at sea level and every lake flat at its own level; rivers follow their valleys down. Streams are off by default: on a big map there are thousands.

River width
1 mm
Water depth
0.6 mm
Pin height
8 mm

Each pin is a map marker — a ball on a cone, point down — set into the ground on a short stem. The cone prints without supports. 0 leaves the pins off.

Mines

Each site gets a small marker whose shape says how far it got: a headframe (a tower with a point) for a working mine, a spoil heap (a low pyramid) for a past producer, a hex stud for a prospect and a dot for a showing. In BC they come from the province's MINFILE; anywhere else from the USGS. Where a district crowds them, the bigger operations win and the rest are left off.

Marker height
4 mm

Forests

The top of the land, wherever it is wooded, in shades of green. In BC each patch is coloured by its leading tree species, from the province's forest inventory: Douglas-fir, hemlock, cedar, spruce and true fir, pine and larch, and broadleaf. Outside BC there is no species data, so the forest is OpenStreetMap's woodland in one green.

Forest depth
0.6 mm

Colours

A pale land colour shows the relief best: the shadows do the work. Each is its own solid, so the 3MF opens with each on its own filament and nothing to paint. Two swatches set to the same colour share one filament.

This map

The STL is every colour welded into one, for a single-colour printer; paint the water and the forest afterwards — their pockets are the outline.

Building… A new place fetches its map first — up to half a minute; changes after that are quicker.
